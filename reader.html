why i made this

hey, i'm vaibhav, and this is simple astro.

i've always loved looking up at the stars, and one of the things i want to do with that passion is make astronomy and astrophysics easier for other people to discover. there is something incredibly exciting about being able to understand what you're seeing in the night sky, and i don't think getting there should require wading through pages of intimidating mathematics before you even know why something matters.

simple astro is my attempt to change that. it is a collection of handouts following the International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics (IOAA) theory syllabus, written to make the ideas as intuitive, approachable, and enjoyable to learn as possible.

a major inspiration for the way these handouts are written is The Feynman Lectures on Physics. i love the way Feynman develops an idea from physical intuition before worrying about formalism. i've tried to bring that same philosophy here, while adding my own way of explaining things, the diagrams, derivations, examples, and problem-solving perspective that helped me understand them in the first place.

the bigger idea

the IOAA syllabus is the framework, but the purpose goes beyond olympiad preparation. i want these handouts to be useful to anyone who wants to learn more about astronomy: a student discovering astrophysics for the first time, an amateur astronomer trying to understand the objects they're observing, or someone who simply looked at the night sky and thought, “i want to know how that works.”

the long-term goal is for simple astro to become a widely used, freely accessible resource for amateur astronomers, astrophysics students, and anyone curious about the universe, wherever they are in the world.

if these notes can make even one person look at the stars a little differently, then they're already doing something worthwhile.

— vaibhav
